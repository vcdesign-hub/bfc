<p>Dear Friends &amp; Family,</p>

<p><strong>Do not become a unicorn startup.</strong><br/></p>

<p>That was my opening advice at a lecture I recently gave at the Technical University of Munich, better known as “<a href="https://www.tum.de/en/" rel="noopener noreferrer" target="_blank">TUM</a>”.</p>

<p>TUM has every right to brag: it <a href="https://www.tum.de/en/news-and-events/all-news/press-releases/details/rang-6-bei-europes-top-unicorn-universities" rel="noopener noreferrer" target="_blank">ranks among those universities</a> with the <strong>highest number of unicorn founders in the world</strong>.</p>

<p>An impressive metric, no doubt.<br/></p>

<p><img alt="Description of Image" class="mx-auto w-full max-w-full rounded-2xl lg:w-[70%]" loading="lazy" src="/images/news/45998/45998-1.jpg"/></p>

<p>But beyond the obvious dilution founders face <strong>when they take too much capital too early</strong>, most people, founders and early-stage investors alike, <a href="https://www.linkedin.com/posts/tech-coast-angels-los-angeles_how-can-a-200m-exit-be-a-failure-activity-7350946815501815810-zL7V/" rel="noopener noreferrer" target="_blank">forget the one thing</a> that can turn your stake into dust when the smallest part of the plan goes wrong (and it usually does): <strong>liquidation preference</strong>.<br/></p>

<p>This <a href="https://www.gsb.stanford.edu/faculty-research/publications/founders-guide-unicorn-creation-how-liquidation-preferences-m" rel="noopener noreferrer" target="_blank">stack sits on top of the cap table</a> like a silent tax waiting to be paid.<br/></p>

<p>Liquidation preference is a threat to unicorn-valued startups because <strong>the headline valuation can feel like fake money</strong> once you look at who gets paid first, how much, and under what conditions.<br/></p>

<p>You can be “worth” €1bn on paper today and <a href="https://www.venturesouth.vc/resources/the-dangers-of-liquidation-preferences" rel="noopener noreferrer" target="_blank">still be structured in a way</a> that only a €2–3bn exit creates real wealth for common shareholders.<br/></p>

<p>Everything in between mainly serves to repay preferred capital.<br/></p>

<p>Which brings me to a broader pattern we’re seeing right now: a <strong>bifurcation in venture funding.</strong><br/></p>

<p>On one side, venture capital is <a href="https://dealpotential.com/vc-2025-fewer-deals-more-money/#:~:text=Global%20venture%20capital%20is%20undergoing,consolidating%20around%20fewer%2C%20larger%20bets." rel="noopener noreferrer" target="_blank">concentrating into fewer, larger bets</a>.<br/></p>

<p>On the other side, <strong>the cost of building something has dropped sharply</strong>.<br/></p>

<p>A team of three, using off-the-shelf models and a modest cloud bill, can now ship in weeks what previously needed dozens of engineers and a full year of burn.<br/></p>

<p><a href="https://www.forbes.com/councils/forbesfinancecouncil/2025/06/10/what-the-biggest-vc-deals-of-q1-2025-reveal-about-the-future-of-investment/?utm_source=chatgpt.com" rel="noopener noreferrer" target="_blank">Capital is clustering</a>; creation is decentralising.<br/></p>

<p>If that sounds like a contradiction, it is only because <strong>the old sequence, raise first, then build, has quietly reversed</strong>.<br/></p>

<p>The new order is to <a href="https://chaoslabs.xyz/posts/build-first-raise-later?utm_source=chatgpt.com" rel="noopener noreferrer" target="_blank">build first</a>, then decide whether to raise. Ten years ago, money bought you the right to experiment.<br/></p>

<p><strong>Today, experimentation is cheap and widely available</strong>; money buys you speed at scale, distribution, and more and more, also the narrative halo that attracts talent and customers.<br/></p>

<p>The question for founders is less “can we get funded?” and more <strong>“is funding really the bottleneck we have?”</strong><br/></p>

<p>There is a historical echo here that the older ones will remember.<br/></p>

<p>In the early internet days, <a href="https://www.forbes.com/councils/forbestechcouncil/2020/01/17/the-speed-of-light-cost-of-bandwidth-and-big-data-whats-really-driving-computing-to-the-edge/?utm_source=chatgpt.com" rel="noopener noreferrer" target="_blank">compute and bandwidth were scarce</a>, so capital flowed to those who could afford servers and sales teams.<br/></p>

<p><strong>Once infrastructure became abundant, value moved up the stack,</strong> to teams who could design products people wanted and iterate faster than big incumbents could organise themselves.<br/></p>

<p>AI is doing something similar now, <strong>shrinking the distance between idea and working prototype</strong>.<br/></p>

<p>The pull of big rounds does not change that. It only shows <strong>how expensive it still is to dominate a market</strong> once you have proven that a market exists.<br/></p>

<p><strong>The winners of this cycle will be those who build early, learn fastest, and raise late, if at all.</strong><br/></p>

<p>As a small early-stage fund, this is exactly what we look for.<br/></p>

<p>Founders who operate in a very capital-efficient way. Those who need little money to prove they are onto something <a href="https://www.ycombinator.com/library/4D-yc-s-essential-startup-advice" rel="noopener noreferrer" target="_blank">that people want</a>.<br/></p>

<p><strong>In public markets, unicorn status is a wonderful thing</strong> (once the lock-up period is over).<br/></p>

<p><strong>In private markets, it can be a burden.</strong><br/></p>

<p>And don’t get me wrong - we love unicorn valuations (especially in our own portfolio).<br/></p>

<p>But we prefer <strong>sustained, controlled growth over a flash-in-the-pan</strong>, driven by enormous customer acquisition costs and with no proven product-market fit.<br/></p>

<p>Just ask the hundreds of <a href="https://onlyfounders.app/all-blogs/zombie-unicorns-trade-wars-the-end-of-startup-delusion" rel="noopener noreferrer" target="_blank">zombie unicorn founders</a>.</p>

<h4>Portfolio Updates</h4>

<p>We are very glad to welcome <a href="https://www.silasinsurtech.com/en" rel="noopener noreferrer" target="_blank">Silas</a>, based in <strong>London</strong>.<br/></p>

<p>Sometimes the best companies start in a simple way: someone looks at a process and thinks, “<strong>There has to be a better way.</strong>”<br/></p>

<p>That is exactly what Silas founder <a href="https://www.linkedin.com/in/maariyaah-afzal/" rel="noopener noreferrer" target="_blank">Maariyaah Afzel</a> thought when she was working as an underwriter.</p>

<p><a href="https://www.bfc.vc/recent-news/why-we-invested-in-silas" rel="noopener noreferrer" target="_blank"><img alt="Description of Image" class="mx-auto w-full max-w-full rounded-2xl lg:w-[70%]" loading="lazy" src="/images/news/45998/45998-2.jpg"/></a></p>

<p><strong>Underwriting is the place where insurers make, or lose, money.</strong><br/></p>

<p>So any technology that improves efficiency, accuracy, and contract certainty goes straight to the bottom line. <strong>A missed clause is not a small error; it can cost millions.</strong><br/></p>

<p>The timing is also right. Insurers are dealing with soft market conditions, a real <a href="https://insuranceblog.accenture.com/why-underwriters-dont-underwrite-much" rel="noopener noreferrer" target="_blank">shortage of underwriting talent</a>, and rising cost pressure. At the same time, <strong>AI adoption has moved from a nice-to-have to a board-level priority.</strong><br/></p>

<p>We are very happy to back Maariyaah alongside <strong>UK fintech veterans</strong> like <a href="https://www.linkedin.com/in/alan-morgan-8b76b119/" rel="noopener noreferrer" target="_blank">Alan Morgan</a> and <a href="https://www.linkedin.com/in/markransford/" rel="noopener noreferrer" target="_blank">Mark Ransford</a>.<br/></p>

<p>Their support underlines the scale of the opportunity and Maariyaah’s unusually <strong>strong founder-market fit</strong>.<br/></p>

<p>And then there’s <a href="https://herita.tech/" rel="noopener noreferrer" target="_blank">herita</a> in Berlin.<br/></p>

<p>Sometimes innovation means inventing something new. Other times, it means <strong>taking a centuries-old idea and rebuilding it for the digital age</strong>.<br/></p>

<p>Herita is doing the latter. They are bringing back one of <strong>the oldest financial instruments in history: the Bill of Exchange</strong>.<br/></p>

<p>When <a href="https://www.linkedin.com/in/christoph-iwaniez/" rel="noopener noreferrer" target="_blank">Christoph Iwaniez</a> first told us about this idea around 12 months ago, in the snowy mountains of Davos, we were intrigued, but we still had a few questions.</p>

<p><a href="https://www.bfc.vc/recent-news/why-we-invested-in-herita" rel="noopener noreferrer" target="_blank"><img alt="Description of Image" class="mx-auto w-full max-w-full rounded-2xl lg:w-[70%]" loading="lazy" src="/images/news/45998/45998-3.webp"/></a></p>

<p>A year later, after getting to know his co-founders <a href="https://www.linkedin.com/in/felixkollmar/" rel="noopener noreferrer" target="_blank">Felix Kollmar</a> and <a href="https://www.linkedin.com/in/benpeterjones/" rel="noopener noreferrer" target="_blank">Ben Jones</a>, and after seeing the <strong>massive interest from potential clients</strong>, we were convinced.<br/></p>

<p>At BFC, we like founders who work at the intersection of the old and the new.<br/></p>

<p>People who understand how things used to function and who can explain why they should function again, just with better infrastructure. Christoph and his colleagues are exactly that type.<br/></p>

<p>They are reactivating an institution of commerce <strong>that outlived empires</strong>, and giving it a <a href="https://www.bfc.vc/recent-news/why-we-invested-in-herita" rel="noopener noreferrer" target="_blank">blockchain-grade reboot</a>.<br/></p>

<p>Because sometimes the future of finance is not about inventing something new.<br/></p>

<p>It is about finally <strong>fixing something that used to work and making it work again</strong>.</p>

<h4>Recommended</h4>

<p><em>“Each wave seduces us into thinking that we’ve learned from history and that this time we can’t be fooled. Then it happens again. This is how it happened in 1929.”</em><br/></p>

<p><strong>Nothing beats a good book</strong> in the quiet year-end holiday season.</p>

<p><a href="https://amzn.to/4rykAIO" rel="noopener noreferrer" target="_blank"><img alt="Description of Image" class="mx-auto w-full max-w-full rounded-2xl lg:w-[70%]" loading="lazy" src="/images/news/45998/45998-4.jpg"/></a></p>

<p>If you are wondering whether AI is a bubble waiting to pop, or simply an exaggeration that will correct itself, you might want to pick up “<a href="https://amzn.to/4rykAIO" rel="noopener noreferrer" target="_blank">1929</a>” by <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrew_Ross_Sorkin" rel="noopener noreferrer" target="_blank">Andrew Ross Sorkin</a>.<br/></p>

<p><strong>When Sorkin writes about that year, it often feels like he is describing 2025.</strong><br/></p>

<p>Against a backdrop of <a href="https://nexusinvestments.com/insight/navigating-the-ai-exuberance-without-losing-our-bearings/" rel="noopener noreferrer" target="_blank">AI exuberance</a>, fast-moving capital, and ever-looser retail access, the book reads almost like a diagnostic tool: <strong>you begin to notice the same psychological patterns and incentive failures</strong> that show up before every major bubble.<br/></p>

<p>For founders and investors, “1929” is both highly entertaining and quietly useful.<br/></p>

<p>It is a reminder that while technology changes, human behaviour in markets barely does.<br/></p>

<p><strong>Markets don’t crash for financial reasons</strong>.<br/></p>

<p>They crash when <a href="https://www.investopedia.com/terms/r/reflexivity.asp?utm_source=chatgpt.com" rel="noopener noreferrer" target="_blank">the narrative breaks</a>.</p>
