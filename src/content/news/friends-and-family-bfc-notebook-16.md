<p>Dear Friends &amp; Family,</p>

<p>In the 1950s, <strong>the Soviet Union had more scientists and engineers</strong> relative to its population than any other major country.<br/></p>

<p>Then came the Sputnik shock(s) of 1957, which, after some introspection, <a href="https://www.popsci.com/sputnik-shocked-world-and-started-space-race/" rel="noopener noreferrer" target="_blank">triggered a robust response</a> from the United States - leading to <strong>increased investment in education and space exploration</strong>.<br/></p>

<p>This moment <strong>ignited the technological</strong> <strong>revolution</strong> that ultimately gave us the Internet, iPhones, Amazon, Google, and ChatGPT, fueling an era of unprecedented economic growth and innovation.</p>

<p><img alt="Description of Image" class="mx-auto w-full max-w-full rounded-2xl lg:w-[70%]" loading="lazy" src="/images/news/45697/45697-1.webp"/></p>

<p><strong>It’s a gift that keeps on giving</strong>, further propelled by the U.S.'s ability to attract the brightest minds in science.</p>

<p>Today, Russia has become mostly irrelevant in scientific advancements, while <strong>China has taken its place</strong>.<br/></p>

<p>The phrase "<strong>China has more honor students than the U.S. has students</strong>" is often cited when comparing the two countries' education systems.<br/></p>

<p>So, could the <a href="https://www.aspistrategist.org.au/deepseek-is-a-modern-sputnik-moment-for-west/" rel="noopener noreferrer" target="_blank">third Sputnik shock</a> - which rattled Western LLM developers in January with the emergence of DeepSeek - trigger a similar response from the U.S.?<br/></p>

<p>After all, there’s <strong>no better remedy for hubris than public humiliation</strong>.<br/></p>

<p>As the <a href="https://fortune.com/2025/01/28/deepseek-deepfake-or-real/" rel="noopener noreferrer" target="_blank">full scope of the DeepSeek</a> story unfolds, it’s remarkable how a single event can shake confidence in a trillion-dollar industry overnight.<br/></p>

<p><strong>Does this also create tailwinds for crypto?</strong><br/></p>

<p>Very much so, and <a href="https://panteracapital.com/" rel="noopener noreferrer" target="_blank">Pantera</a> captured the essence of the moment perfectly:<br/></p>

<p><em>"Imagine the state of the Internet if Jeff Bezos went to jail for online book sales. If Steve Jobs were sanctioned for launching the App Store? Or, if Jensen Huang was forced to build Nvidia outside of the US because Operation Chokepoint closed his bank account? That's the twilight zone that our industry is emerging from."</em><br/></p>

<p>If the U.S. truly <a href="https://www.cnbc.com/2025/02/04/trump-crypto-czar-david-sacks-says-priority-is-stablecoin-legislation.html#:~:text=As%20David%20Sacks%2C%20the%20newly,be%20focused%20on%20is%20stablecoins." rel="noopener noreferrer" target="_blank">embraces crypto deregulation</a>, it won’t just be a domestic shift - it will have repercussions on global markets, <strong>forcing other nations to recalibrate their approach</strong>.<br/></p>

<p>Talk about <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isoroku_Yamamoto%27s_sleeping_giant_quote" rel="noopener noreferrer" target="_blank">waking up a sleeping giant</a>.</p>

<h4>Recommended</h4>

<p>There’s <strong>no doubt that blockchain technology will become the technological backbone of the financial industry</strong> and an integral part of most modern tech stacks.<br/></p>

<p>But Bitcoin—that’s a different story. Or is it?<br/></p>

<p>In preparation for our Davos event, which once again featured an opening keynote by <strong>Anthony Scaramucci</strong>, I decided to read his new book, “<a href="https://amzn.to/40LyLh5" rel="noopener noreferrer" target="_blank">The Little Book of Bitcoin: What You Need to Know that Wall Street Has Already Figured Out</a>”.<br/></p>

<p>Most books about Bitcoin are rather boring, recycling the same old narratives about banking the unbanked and democratization.<br/></p>

<p>But Anthony Scaramucci’s book is different. <strong>It’s about psychology and shifting paradigms.</strong></p>

<p><img alt="Description of Image" class="mx-auto w-full max-w-full rounded-2xl lg:w-[70%]" loading="lazy" src="/images/news/45697/45697-6.webp"/></p>

<p><em>“I think the punchline gets missed with everyone focusing on Bitcoin. The revolution is really about moving to the decentralized world.”</em><br/></p>

<p>He shares how Wall Street slowly came to embrace Bitcoin, <strong>how he was ridiculed and lost many investors in his hedge fund</strong> - people who thought he had lost his mind when he pivoted to crypto.<br/></p>

<p>And how, last year, <a href="https://pomp.substack.com/p/anthony-scaramuccis-skybridge-outperformed" rel="noopener noreferrer" target="_blank">SkyBridge’s flagship fund outperformed</a> many of Wall Street’s biggest hedge funds, including Tiger Global, Citadel, and Pershing Square.<br/></p>

<p><strong>Whatever your personal conclusion</strong>, it never hurts to read <a href="https://amzn.to/40LyLh5" rel="noopener noreferrer" target="_blank">a good book</a>.</p>
