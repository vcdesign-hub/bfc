<p>Dear Friends &amp; Family,</p>

<p>When Steve Jobs <a href="https://bfc.us8.list-manage.com/track/click?u=b24a4a180be6b26d2e7ed0cdb&amp;id=8cb1b9dc79&amp;e=5e776130b3" rel="noopener noreferrer" target="_blank">unveiled the iPhone in June 2007</a>, he famously introduced it as not one, but three revolutionary products: a touchscreen iPod, a mobile phone, and an Internet communicator.<br/></p>

<p>Of course, it wasn’t three devices.<br/></p>

<p>It was one.</p>

<p><a href="https://bfc.us8.list-manage.com/track/click?u=b24a4a180be6b26d2e7ed0cdb&amp;id=753801688f&amp;e=5e776130b3" rel="noopener noreferrer" target="_blank"><img alt="Description of Image"  loading="lazy" src="/images/news/45770/45770-1.png"/></a></p>

<p>But over time, it became something <a href="https://bfc.us8.list-manage.com/track/click?u=b24a4a180be6b26d2e7ed0cdb&amp;id=ba6f5baad6&amp;e=5e776130b3" rel="noopener noreferrer" target="_blank">closer to a hundred</a>.<br/></p>

<p>The iPhone didn’t just combine functions—<strong>it absorbed entire categories</strong>.<br/></p>

<p>It rendered standalone GPS units, digital cameras, MP3 players, and even handheld gaming consoles increasingly obsolete.<br/></p>

<p>For companies building those devices, the iPhone wasn’t innovation.<br/></p>

<p>It was extinction.<br/></p>

<p>Fast forward fifteen years, and ChatGPT &amp; Co. <a href="https://bfc.us8.list-manage.com/track/click?u=b24a4a180be6b26d2e7ed0cdb&amp;id=3c9981b82e&amp;e=5e776130b3" rel="noopener noreferrer" target="_blank">are playing a similar game</a>—this time in software.<br/></p>

<p>What began as a text generator quickly evolved into an all-purpose assistant: answering questions, drafting content, helping with code, and more.<br/></p>

<p>Like the iPhone, ChatGPT and its successors didn’t just compete—<strong>they collapsed boundaries between tools.</strong><br/></p>

<p>Industries built around niche capabilities are now facing an uncomfortable question: what happens when your product becomes a feature in someone else’s platform?<br/></p>

<p>Imagine you’ve spent a decade building the world’s leading translation service. Or you’re a copywriter, an illustrator, a data entry specialist.<br/></p>

<p>Just ask <a href="https://bfc.us8.list-manage.com/track/click?u=b24a4a180be6b26d2e7ed0cdb&amp;id=62aab803c6&amp;e=5e776130b3" rel="noopener noreferrer" target="_blank">Quora</a>, which is struggling to stay relevant as users flock to ChatGPT or Grok for instant, conversational answers.<br/></p>

<p>Or <a href="https://bfc.us8.list-manage.com/track/click?u=b24a4a180be6b26d2e7ed0cdb&amp;id=0586604cb1&amp;e=5e776130b3" rel="noopener noreferrer" target="_blank">Stack Overflow</a>, once the undisputed go-to for developers, now fighting for attention in an era where AI writes cleaner code in less time.<br/></p>

<p>These were billion-dollar platforms.<br/></p>

<p>Emphasis on were.<br/></p>

<p>I predict that large software development companies, such as Accenture or Capgemini, will <a href="https://bfc.us8.list-manage.com/track/click?u=b24a4a180be6b26d2e7ed0cdb&amp;id=a0a5a19959&amp;e=5e776130b3" rel="noopener noreferrer" target="_blank">initiate mass layoffs</a> this year.<br/></p>

<p>No human can compete with the speed of tools like <a href="https://bfc.us8.list-manage.com/track/click?u=b24a4a180be6b26d2e7ed0cdb&amp;id=9c45e57c4c&amp;e=5e776130b3" rel="noopener noreferrer" target="_blank">Cursor</a>, <a href="https://bfc.us8.list-manage.com/track/click?u=b24a4a180be6b26d2e7ed0cdb&amp;id=b900e6f77e&amp;e=5e776130b3" rel="noopener noreferrer" target="_blank">Replit</a>, or <a href="https://bfc.us8.list-manage.com/track/click?u=b24a4a180be6b26d2e7ed0cdb&amp;id=569c86cf08&amp;e=5e776130b3" rel="noopener noreferrer" target="_blank">Lovable</a>.<br/></p>

<p>What once took teams of hundreds or even thousands will soon be executed by ten highly skilled software architects armed with AI copilots.<br/></p>

<p><strong>This also completely changes the venture capital game.</strong><br/></p>

<p>Many software startups built in the last 5-10 years will struggle to remain relevant when AI seemingly can do everything.<br/></p>

<p>Unless your software is <strong>deeply integrated with your client's business</strong> and tied to compliance, certification, specific industry insights, internal workforce agreements, etc., there is a risk that it will be replaced with an AI-native tool.<br/></p>

<p><a href="https://bfc.us8.list-manage.com/track/click?u=b24a4a180be6b26d2e7ed0cdb&amp;id=a376b03e0e&amp;e=5e776130b3" rel="noopener noreferrer" target="_blank">Tech moat will become less relevant</a>, go-to-market strategies will make all the difference.<br/></p>

<p>In short, there's never been a time when building and scaling a software company required less capital, or when surviving as a legacy product required more edge.<br/></p>

<p>And as a microfund, <strong>we think that’s a good thing</strong>.</p>
