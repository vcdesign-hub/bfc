<p>When you pitch a service to a client, and they decline but offer to invest in your company instead, you know you're onto something special.</p>

<p>That's exactly what happened to <a href="https://www.linkedin.com/in/raechel-lambert/" rel="noopener noreferrer" target="_blank">Rae Lambert</a>, co-founder and CEO of River, a company on a mission to turn online audiences into thriving communities.</p>

<p>When she pitched her marketing services to <strong>Jason Calacanis</strong>, the American internet entrepreneur and investor famous for his "<strong>All-In" podcast</strong> with Chamath Palihapitiya, David Sacks, and David Friedberg, he wanted to invest instead.</p>

<p>Shortly after, <strong>Balaji Srinivasan</strong>, the renowned investor and Web3 entrepreneur, also wanted to get in.</p>

<p>That’s when Rae and her team decided to double down on their community-building magic called River.</p>

<p>6 months later, she presented her startup during a demo day of <a href="https://www.launch.co/" rel="noopener noreferrer" target="_blank">Jason Calacanis' Launch Accelerator</a> and <strong>was voted best startup</strong>.</p>

<p>This is where we discovered the team.</p>

<p>We thought we knew the event organization space well. After all, there is Luma and many other tools.</p>

<p>Still, when we dug deeper, we discovered that Rae, her husband, <a href="https://www.linkedin.com/in/ryanglambert/" rel="noopener noreferrer" target="_blank">Ryan Lambert</a>, and their colleague <a href="https://www.linkedin.com/in/bernii/" rel="noopener noreferrer" target="_blank">Berni Kobos</a> were doing <strong>something completely different</strong> and, dare we say, revolutionary.</p>

<h4>Life is all about the things we have in common</h4>

<p>Think of River as an <strong>operating system for community building</strong>. </p>

<p>It's Cult(ure) as a Service.</p>

<p>What do the largest communities have in common? </p>

<p>They get people all around the world to spread the gospel (sometimes literally) about products, methods, sports, faith, and much more, often without having to pay them a dime.</p>

<p>If someone loves the Apple brand, they'll share their passion like a tech evangelist. </p>

<p>If you've found inner peace through religion, you'll want to share it and start a group to discuss it. </p>

<p>If you're Nike, you want people to run, jump, and sweat together while sporting your gear.</p>

<p><strong>The world is built on communities</strong>. </p>

<p>COVID-19 brought a brutal stop to many real-life activities for almost two years, and loneliness numbers are still higher than before the pandemic.</p>

<p>Also, digitalization increasingly disrupts existing social touchpoints, such as universities or workplaces, where many friendships and relationships have been formed.</p>

<p>If you're an influencer or a brand, <strong>you want people to talk about you</strong>, share their experiences, and spread the word about your products. </p>

<p>But organizing a community? </p>

<p>It's like herding cats - adorable but a total nightmare. The key is to <strong>scale while retaining the quality of your mission</strong>. It's not easy.</p>

<p>This is where River comes in. </p>

<h4>The River platform makes it easy</h4>

<p>River is a SaaS platform designed to <strong>massively reduce the workload for brands and influencers</strong> organizing and orchestrating global communities.</p>

<p><img alt="Description of Image" class="mx-auto w-full max-w-full rounded-2xl lg:w-[70%]" loading="lazy" src="/images/news/45469-2/45469-2-1.png"/></p>

<p>It helps you find the right local representatives (read: super fans) to manage local events while <strong>ensuring guidelines</strong> are followed. </p>

<p>You can create 100+ branded events with a fraction of the effort it would take if you used traditional meeting tools.</p>

<p><strong>Bryan Johnson</strong> already uses River to organize his “<a href="https://dontdie.bryanjohnson.com/" rel="noopener noreferrer" target="_blank">Don’t Die</a>” longevity mission. </p>

<p>Jason Calacanis uses it to orchestrate local events for his “This Week in Startups” and “All-In” podcasts.</p>

<p>If the Catholic Church had been founded today, we believe they would use River as their operating system to run their global flock. And this is just the beginning.</p>

<h4>The blockchain angle</h4>

<p><strong>Physical meetings are crucial</strong> for talking to developers and creating momentum for new blockchains and applications, especially in the crypto world. </p>

<p>NFTs can serve as <strong>gatekeepers</strong> to grant entry to certain events, and wallets carrying specific credentials can supercharge the recruitment process of local hosts. </p>

<p>And there is a lot more to come.</p>
