<p>Dear Friend and Family,<br/></p>

<p>We’re just nine days away from the <a href="https://elections.mywoodcounty.com/elections-office/election-fun-facts/" rel="noopener noreferrer" target="_blank">US election</a>, and as usual, we are being bombarded with <strong>polls predicting the outcome.</strong></p>

<p><img alt="U.S Election" class="mx-auto w-full max-w-full rounded-2xl lg:w-[70%]" loading="lazy" src="/images/news/45592/45592-1.jpeg"/></p>

<p>Given the questionable accuracy of these polls in recent elections, I’ve stopped paying much attention. <br/></p>

<p>Their track record has been less than stellar.<br/></p>

<p>One platform that has caught my eye, however, is <a href="https://polymarket.com/" rel="noopener noreferrer" target="_blank">Polymarket</a>—the <strong>blockchain-based prediction market</strong>. <br/></p>

<p>Though it's banned for US citizens, it has nonetheless gained significant attention, particularly for political betting.<br/></p>

<p><a href="https://www.investopedia.com/terms/p/prediction-market.asp" rel="noopener noreferrer" target="_blank">Prediction markets</a> are often praised for their effectiveness, as they <strong>incentivize knowledgeable insiders</strong> to put their money where their mouth is. <br/></p>

<p>And Polymarket was initially hailed as the ultimate prediction tool because people <strong>had to invest real (crypto) money</strong> to participate.<br/></p>

<p>That was until last week.</p>

<p><a href="https://polymarket.com/" rel="noopener noreferrer" target="_blank"><img alt="Polymarket" class="mx-auto w-full max-w-full rounded-2xl lg:w-[70%]" loading="lazy" src="/images/news/45592/45592-2.png"/></a></p>

<p>It was <a href="https://www.reddit.com/r/Askpolitics/comments/1g5bxy7/why_did_trump_winning_betting_odds_suddenly_spike/" rel="noopener noreferrer" target="_blank">revealed that four anonymous large traders</a>, or "whales," placed $30 million worth of bets on Trump, causing his odds of winning to skyrocket.<br/></p>

<p>Clearly, Polymarket's founders overlooked one key factor: <strong>people may have motives beyond financial gain when placing bets.</strong><br/></p>

<p>So, we’re left waiting for the early morning hours of November 6.<br/></p>

<p>Or maybe <a href="https://www.history.com/news/2000-election-bush-gore-votes-supreme-court" rel="noopener noreferrer" target="_blank">even longer</a>.<br/></p>

<p>Fortunately, the founding fathers built in a two-month buffer between Election Day and the inauguration. <br/></p>

<p>We might just need it—blockchain prediction models notwithstanding.</p>

<h4>How to tell if a founder team will succeed</h4>

<p>That’s the trillion-dollar question every venture firm wishes had a simple answer.<br/></p>

<p>As some of you may know, we collect <a href="https://entrepreneurscan.com/blog/psychometric-tests-for-entrepreneurs/" rel="noopener noreferrer" target="_blank">psychometric assessments</a> from our founders. <br/></p>

<p>Our aim is to <strong>better understand team dynamics, communication styles, and individual preferences</strong>, while also identifying patterns that make us even better at spotting great investments in the future.</p>

<p><img alt="Psychometric Assesment" class="mx-auto w-full max-w-full rounded-2xl lg:w-[70%]" loading="lazy" src="/images/news/45592/45592-3.png"/></p>

<p>There are <strong>no right or wrong answers</strong> in these assessments, and we openly discuss the results with our founders—sharing our own profiles with them as well.<br/></p>

<p>Unlike popular assessments like the <a href="https://www.16personalities.com/free-personality-test" rel="noopener noreferrer" target="_blank">Big Five or MBTI</a>, we take it a step further.<br/></p>

<p>We look at <strong>more than fourty traits</strong> to get a deeper insight into what makes each team tick.<br/></p>

<p>To date, we’ve made <a href="https://www.bfc.vc/portfolio" rel="noopener noreferrer" target="_blank">sixteen investments</a> through our fund, fourteen of which have been disclosed, with two more to be announced in the coming weeks.<br/></p>

<p>Two key themes have emerged from our analysis: <strong>early life adversity and migration</strong>.<br/></p>

<p><strong>Founders who faced significant challenges</strong> growing up—whether it be financial hardship, frequent relocations, familial instability, or even bullying at school—tend to develop resilience. <br/></p>

<p>And without a lot of resilience and grit, it’s hard to enjoy the emotional roller-coaster of startup life.<br/></p>

<p>Discussing these sensitive topics during early interviews <strong>can be tricky</strong>. <br/></p>

<p>Yet, more often than not, we’re pleasantly surprised to find that <strong>strong founder types wear their turbulent upbringing as a badge of honor</strong>, rather than carrying negative feelings about it.<br/></p>

<p>And whether it’s a German kid moving to the U.S. like Peter Thiel, an Iranian-American CEO like Uber’s <a href="https://www.uber.com/us/en/about/leadership/dara-khosrowshahi/" rel="noopener noreferrer" target="_blank">Dara Khosrowshahi</a>, or one of the <a href="https://x.com/sagarbarvaliya/status/1829475201085559218" rel="noopener noreferrer" target="_blank">many Indians</a> leading the tech world, there’s plenty of evidence that immigrant founders, <strong>many of whom faced significant hurdles upon arriving in new countries</strong>, are more likely to succeed.</p>

<p><a href="https://x.com/sagarbarvaliya/status/1829475201085559218" rel="noopener noreferrer" target="_blank"><img alt="Indian Immigrants" class="mx-auto w-full max-w-full rounded-2xl lg:w-[70%]" loading="lazy" src="/images/news/45592/45592-4.png"/></a></p>

<p>According to a <a href="https://www.forbes.com/sites/stuartanderson/2022/07/26/most-us-billion-dollar-startups-have-an-immigrant-founder/" rel="noopener noreferrer" target="_blank">2022 Forbes article</a>, nearly two-thirds (64%) of U.S. billion-dollar companies were founded or cofounded by immigrants or the children of immigrants.<br/></p>

<p>There’s no singular “perfect” founder profile. <br/></p>

<p>But those who fight every day, refuse to take "no" for an answer, and motivate their troops during tough times tend to fare better, especially when they have <strong>no Plan B or soft cushion to fall back on</strong>.</p>

<p><strong>Perspective</strong> also plays a key role. <br/></p>

<p>The ability to distinguish between a real crisis and a challenge that can be overcome with hard work is often the difference between success and failure.</p>

<h4>Recommended<br/></h4>

<p>Long-time early-stage VC <a href="https://www.floodgate.com/team/mike-maples-jr" rel="noopener noreferrer" target="_blank"><strong>Mike Maples</strong></a> estimates that 80% of his fund’s returns come from startups that <strong>pivoted at least once</strong>, if not multiple times.<br/></p>

<p>What’s even more critical, however, is seeing an inflection point <strong>where something new suddenly becomes possible</strong>, along with the founders' insight into how this will reshape the future.</p>

<p><a href="https://amzn.to/3YdtFZM" rel="noopener noreferrer" target="_blank"><img alt="Mike Males Jr &amp; Peter Ziebelman" class="mx-auto w-full max-w-full rounded-2xl lg:w-[70%]" loading="lazy" src="/images/news/45592/45592-7.jpg"/></a></p>

<p>“The product itself during the seed stage is <strong>just a reference implementation</strong> of that insight,” says Maples. “But the <strong>insight itself should not change</strong>.”<br/></p>

<p>One of the best explanations of what a seed fund is truly investing in can be found in his book, “<a href="https://amzn.to/3YdtFZM" rel="noopener noreferrer" target="_blank">Pattern Breakers</a>”, which is <strong>based on his analysis of why some startups in his portfolio succeeded while others didn't</strong>.<br/></p>

<p>“The incumbents will fight unfairly if the startup doesn’t do something to <strong>change the rules and force a new playing field</strong>.”<br/></p>

<p>And my favorite quote from his book: “<strong>Make your product a choice, not a comparison</strong>.”<br/></p>

<p>After all, when was the last time you compared the technical specs of an iPhone to another brand?<br/></p>

<p>If you don’t have a rainy weekend to read the book, there’s also an excellent <a href="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=b_lqGHKHi5M" rel="noopener noreferrer" target="_blank">YouTube interview</a> where Maples discusses his findings.</p>
