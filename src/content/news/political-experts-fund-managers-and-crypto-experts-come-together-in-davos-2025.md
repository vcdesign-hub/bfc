<p><img alt="Description of Image"  loading="lazy" src="/images/news/45678/45678-1.webp"/></p>

<p>The annual Web3 Investor Gathering at the Seehof Hotel in Davos, <strong>co-hosted by Blockchain Founders Capital and Animoca Brands</strong>, brought together an illustrious group of political experts, fund managers, and crypto experts to discuss why 2025 is poised to become a <strong>year of mainstream adoption of blockchain technologies</strong>.</p>

<p>With President Donald Trump's inauguration just the day before, there were many discussions about his executive orders regarding crypto and how US regulation will finally become a key driver of mass adoption.</p>

<p><strong>Anthony Scaramucci</strong>, founder and managing partner of SkyBridge, kicked off the session with his keynote address: </p>

<p>“<em>When you get Donald Trump, you get everything. You get the meme coins, you get the 100% tariffs, you get the bellicosity of the rhetoric.</em>”</p>

<p>“<em>But like or dislike Donald Trump, </em><strong><em>he understood that a big part of this industry needs to be in the United States</em></strong><em>.</em>”</p>

<p>In a fireside chat with Wolfgang Männel, <strong>Yat Siu</strong>, chairman and co-founder of Animoca Brands, said, “<em>AI, and particularly tokenization and blockchain, is so relevant because </em><strong><em>it's the native digital currency of essentially the digital world</em></strong><em>, which is what an AI is.</em>”</p>

<p><img alt="Description of Image"  loading="lazy" src="/images/news/45678/45678-2.webp"/></p>

<p>“<em>I see digital </em><strong><em>identity as the basis for building a reputation layer</em></strong><em>. The reputation industry powers trillions of dollars and provides a much more inclusive economy.</em>”</p>

<p><strong>Olav Chen</strong>, Head of Storebrand Asset Management’s team, a leading financial player in the Nordic market, said, “<em>Web3 will impact our industry, which is a</em> <em>dinosaur today, in many ways, starting with near-time clearing and settlement processes.</em>”</p>

<p><strong>Himanshu Gulati</strong>, a Norwegian member of Parliament, adds, “<em>We politicians are often slow to</em> <em>understand the impact of new technologies because we are too busy with other challenges.</em> <em>That’s why we have invited successful blockchain companies to speak in the Norwegian</em> <em>parliament to educate us.</em>”</p>

<p><strong>Nitin Gaur</strong>, founder and CTO of LedgerLink, hosted a panel with <strong>Annelise Osborne</strong> from Kadena, <strong>Philipp Vonmoos</strong> from Solana, <strong>Gordon Liao</strong> from Circle, and <strong>Porter Stowell</strong> from Filecoin Foundation on “Blockchain Infrastructure and Stablecoins for Institutional DeFi” and said, “<em>Corporates right now just transfer about </em><strong><em>$23 trillion in cross border payments</em></strong><em>. JP Morgan estimates that $120 billion was spent on fees. Now, that's not even currency risk. There is a considerable cost benefit to be captured.</em>“</p>

<p><img alt="Description of Image"  loading="lazy" src="/images/news/45678/45678-3.webp"/></p>

<p><strong>Evan Auyang</strong>, president of Animoca Brands, talked about the company's transformation from a previous focus on gaming startups to a <strong>digital version of an investment bank</strong> providing digital asset advisory and banking services. </p>

<p><img alt="Description of Image"  loading="lazy" src="/images/news/45678/45678-4.webp"/></p>

<p><strong>Alan Lau</strong>, chief business officer of Animoca Brands, spoke about AI agents and blockchain, saying, “<em>I think the real innovation comes when crypto is involved. You're giving wings to AI agents when you have something that can </em><strong><em>act autonomously but with money</em></strong><em>. In some way, crypto was made for machines. It wasn't made for humans.</em>”</p>

<p><img alt="Description of Image"  loading="lazy" src="/images/news/45678/45678-5.webp"/></p>

<p><strong>Bibin Babu</strong>, co-founder of Paycio, hosted a panel with Laura Inamedinova from Gate.io, Gopi Kannappan from bitsCrunch, Prakash Somosundram from AYA, and Constantin Kogan from TDX on “The Future of Finance”. </p>

<p>He said, “<em>560 million people currently have crypto exposure, and less than 8 million actually use decentralized finance. If we want more people to use crypto, </em><strong><em>we</em></strong><em> </em><strong><em>must improve the user experience significantly</em></strong><em>.</em>”</p>

<p><img alt="Description of Image"  loading="lazy" src="/images/news/45678/45678-6.webp"/></p>

<p><strong>Terence Kwok</strong>, founder of Humanity Protocol, talked about reclaiming digital identity to reduce fraud, saying, “<em>We think having sort of a </em><strong><em>credential ability on the blockchain</em></strong><em> will unlock a lot of opportunity in this space.</em>” In the case of Humanity Protocol, this is based on <strong>biometrics, the scan of your palm</strong>.</p>

<p><img alt="Description of Image"  loading="lazy" src="/images/news/45678/45678-7.webp"/></p>

<p><strong>Vivek Anand Oberoi</strong>, the famous Indian actor turned businessman, says, “<em>I believe in blockchain. It works. However, there's a trust deficit with Web3, which needs to be communicated by utility. And </em><strong><em>with mass adoption will come mass investments</em></strong><em>. Right now, you're still looking at limited pools of capital. But this can change very quickly.</em>”</p>

<p><img alt="Description of Image"  loading="lazy" src="/images/news/45678/45678-8.webp"/></p>

<p><strong>Vijay Shekhar Sharma</strong>, the founder of Paytm who took his company public at a $19 billion valuation in 2021, said, “<em>Stablecoins are probably the best device for sorting out the crossborder India receives. The largest percentage of that comes from people remitting to India. That's probably $120 billion last year. So, </em><strong><em>remitting at a lower cost by leveraging Stablecoin</em></strong><em> would be a fascinating use case.</em>”</p>

<p><img alt="Description of Image"  loading="lazy" src="/images/news/45678/45678-9.webp"/></p>

<p><strong>Sagar Barvaliya</strong> stated: “This was the best lineup of speakers we've ever had, combining <strong>battle-hardened entrepreneurs, savvy global investors, and renowned Web3 experts</strong>. This forum is a treasure for know-how exchange, allowing us to learn from each other's successes as well as from the things that didn’t go as planned. And this is what Davos is all about.</p>

<p>The Davos Web3 Investor Gathering <strong>will return in 2026</strong>. </p>
