<p><em>Philipp Sandner, researcher, community organizer, investor, and co-founder of Blockchain Founders Capital, died on January 16, 2024, aged 43.</em></p>

<p><strong>Patents</strong> first captured his attention when he wrote his 2009 dissertation on "The Valuation of Intangible Assets - An Exploration of Patent and Trademark Portfolios."</p>

<p>Later, his research shifted to another nonphysical domain: <strong>bitcoins and blockchains</strong>. But he never failed to make the transfer from academic studies to the real-life business world.</p>

<p>Although his subject areas were intangible, <strong>he remained very approachable as a professor</strong>, community organizer, mentor, investor, founder, adviser, and thought leader.</p>

<p>Well before becoming the <strong>gravitational center of the German blockchain community</strong>, he <strong>co-founded his first startup in 2011</strong> with a focus on patent monetization.</p>

<p>He believed that patented inventions needed the right context to maximize their potential. To achieve this, he connected inventors with tech companies, brokering mutually beneficial technology transfers.</p>

<p><strong>Then he discovered Bitcoin</strong>, the first use case of blockchain technology, and became curious.</p>

<p>After 2016, as global intellectual property markets nosedived due to legal disputes between Apple and Samsung, he moved all in and <strong>founded the Frankfurt School Blockchain Center</strong>.</p>

<p>This mission is what most people would come to remember him for.</p>

<p>He realized businesses needed a clear understanding of blockchain technology to embrace its possibilities fully. </p>

<p>To achieve that, he never hid behind the special lingo and complexities of the crypto world but instead <strong>found simple ways to explain his fascination with the technology</strong>.</p>

<p>He held strong convictions about where the technology would eventually go but was often frustrated by the industry's slow pace of adoption.</p>

<p>He appreciated regulation because it legitimized his research area a little more every year. However, he <strong>perceived far greater potential in this technology</strong> than most politicians or industry leaders could see.</p>

<p>He went on to <strong>build the German blockchain community</strong> step by step through educational programs, podcasts, round-tables, conferences, chat groups, books, articles, and TV appearances. </p>

<p>He never put himself in the spotlight but advocated for the cause of the research area he passionately stood for.</p>

<p>Noticing that only a small fraction of his blockchain students were women, he initiated the “<strong>DLT Talents</strong>” program at the Frankfurt School, paving the way for many of today's female leaders in the crypto world.</p>

<p>Despite his strong convictions, he remained self-reflective, often asking his friends, “Are we on the right track?” </p>

<p>He never shied away from seeking and hearing their sometimes uncomfortable but honest opinions and would act on the feedback.</p>

<p><strong>Networking was his forte</strong>. He brought people together. Typically, if you sought his help, you would leave with at least two recommended contacts he personally provided.</p>

<p>While not exactly an extrovert, he felt at ease with people from all walks of life. Always a bit reserved, he would shake hands where others hugged. Yet <strong>his esteem for the other person was always honest, heartfelt, and respectful</strong>.</p>

<p><strong>Productivity was his mantra</strong>. To manage his massive workload, he “cloned” himself by sharing his best practices, software tools, and processes with his team. “There wasn’t a keyboard shortcut he didn’t know,” remarked a former colleague.</p>

<p>He had learned from the patent space that having <strong>an idea was worthless unless you could turn it into a real product or a service</strong>.</p>

<p>Therefore, whenever a startup team approached him with a new idea, he insisted they launch a website within four weeks. He also strongly advised them to talk with potential customers daily, well before they had a finished product.</p>

<p>He knew many ideas would not survive the reality check of talking to clients.</p>

<p>Long before it became fashionable again to focus on generating revenue over raising venture capital, he <strong>advised his young founders to start earning revenue</strong>, no matter how small, right from the start.</p>

<p>Bragging about his numerous achievements, whether in academia or business, was not his style. Yet, he appreciated the respect it showed. He was acutely aware, however, that such <strong>respect had to be earned anew each day</strong>.</p>

<p>Although he appeared to be working 24 hours a day, everyone in his circle of friends knew that his family was the achievement he was proudest of.</p>

<p>He would be the first to concede that going on a hike in the Alps with his family was <strong>so much better than any crypto Metaverse </strong>could ever be<strong>.</strong></p>
